The Problem

India's Cultural Heritage Is Disappearing Faster Than It Is Being Digitally Preserved

Thousands of art forms, oral traditions and craft techniques survive only in scattered records and living memory — with no unified system to document or prioritise them.

🗂️

Fragmented Documentation

Cultural knowledge is scattered across archives, institutions and local communities, with no single searchable record.

🎙️

Disappearing Oral Traditions

Stories, songs, rituals and indigenous knowledge are passed down verbally and risk disappearing with each generation.

📡

Limited Digital Visibility

Many local artisans and cultural communities lack a strong digital presence, limiting recognition and support.

🧭

Lack of Preservation Intelligence

There is no simple, unified interface for understanding which heritage practices need urgent documentation and preservation.

💡
BharatVerse's response: a single workflow where communities contribute heritage knowledge, technology assists in organising it, experts verify it, and preservation needs are visualised through an interactive heritage intelligence system — instead of another static information website.

Preservation Intelligence

Heritage Preservation Risk Analysis

A prototype signal — not a scientific or government-verified score — that helps prioritise which heritage practices need urgent documentation.

ⓘ What is the Preservation Priority Indicator?
Score = 40% Transmission Risk (master's age ÷ active apprentices — the clearest signal of whether a skill is actually passing on) + 25% documentation gap + 20% weak digital presence + 15% low youth participation. Demo values here are illustrative; a production version would pull these from verified institutional and community datasets.
